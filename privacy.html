SecureChat Privacy Policy

Version 1.1 · Effective 12 November 2026

In short: your messages are end-to-end encrypted, pass through our server only while both phones are online, and are never stored there. The server keeps a random account number and what it needs to check the app is genuine, nothing that names you. There are no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no push notifications.

Who is responsible

SecureChat is made by Simone Barbara, who is responsible for the data described here. For any question about this policy or your data, write to securechat.private.messenger at proton.me.

What stays on your phone

  • Your SecureChat contacts: the people you have added inside the app, in person (see "Adding a contact"). SecureChat never reads your iPhone's Contacts or address book. For each one: their name, their public key, the name and notes you give them, and when they're usually around.
  • Your messages, and your own name and the times you're usually around.
  • Messages are encrypted on the phone with a key for each contact; the keys and your identity key are in the iPhone's Keychain, on this device only. The database is excluded from iCloud and device backups.
  • Deleting a chat or a contact deletes its key first, so what's left can never be read again.

The server keeps no names, phone numbers, email addresses or messages, and writes no contacts to disk.

The SecureChat servers' logs record events (a connection, a refusal, an error) without IP addresses, account numbers or message content. The server's operating system keeps its firewall and administrator-login records, which include IP addresses, for 14 days; using the app doesn't add to them.

With only a few people using SecureChat, the server's load every 5 minutes shows roughly when people are chatting, though not who.

Messages and being online

  • Messages travel through the relay end-to-end encrypted, padded to one size, and only while both phones have SecureChat open. If your contact isn't there, the message is not sent and nothing is queued on the server.
  • To deliver them, the relay sees which two account numbers are talking, while they talk. It keeps this in memory only, for the length of the connection.
  • When a message reaches your contact's phone, their phone sends back a small, encrypted receipt, so your app can tell you if a message didn't arrive. Your contact's app learns the same about your phone. The relay can't read a receipt, but from its timing it can tell a receipt from a message someone typed.
  • While SecureChat is open on your phone, your contacts can see that you're available, and when you leave.
  • Contacts you have blocked or deleted, and anyone else, can't reach you or see whether you're online: to them you always look away.
  • When you're usually around reaches only your contacts, end-to-end encrypted. Blocked contacts receive no messages and no times from you.

What we can't prevent: whoever runs or takes over the server could watch, while it happens, which accounts are talking, how much and when, but could not read what they say.

Adding a contact

Contacts are added in person: each phone scans the other's code. The code holds your public key, your account number and the name you chose; no server is involved. The camera is used only for this scan. Anyone who photographs your code learns your account number and public key, but can't reach you unless you add them too.

Why we may use this data

  • To provide the service you asked for: registration, connecting, delivering messages and receipts, and deleting your account (performance of a contract).
  • To keep the service secure and working: checking the app is genuine, limiting abuse, backups, the daily totals and the server's load, answering your emails, and using TestFlight feedback and crash reports to fix problems (our legitimate interest in running a safe and reliable service).

The server needs this data to work; without it the app can't be used. No decisions about you are made automatically.

Who else is involved

  • Apple: App Attest confirms that the app is genuine, and Apple distributes the app (TestFlight, App Store) under its own privacy policy.
  • Infomaniak hosts the server in its own data centres in Switzerland, a country the European Union recognises as protecting personal data adequately.
  • Let's Encrypt issues the server's HTTPS certificate.
  • DuckDNS provides the server's name for now, and so answers lookups of that name (usually from your internet provider, not from your phone).
  • Proton (Proton AG, Switzerland) stores the emails you send us.
  • GitHub hosts this page; like any website host, it sees the address of whoever visits it.

If you send feedback through TestFlight, or allow your iPhone to share analytics with app developers, Apple passes us that feedback and crash reports, which include your device model and iOS version. For TestFlight testers, Apple also shows us the name and email address the invitation was sent to. We delete emails and TestFlight feedback once they're dealt with, and at the latest after 12 months. Apple keeps crash reports in App Store Connect under its own terms.

No data is sold or shared with anyone else, and the app contains no third-party analytics, advertising, crash reporting or tracking.

Deleting your data

  • Delete Account, in the app under Me, deletes your account record on the server at once, then everything on your phone: messages, contacts and keys. A session token already issued expires within 15 minutes. Encrypted backups that still hold the record are deleted within 14 days.
  • An account whose app hasn't connected for 12 months is deleted automatically.
  • Deleting SecureChat from your phone without Delete Account removes your chats and contacts. Its keys in the iPhone's Keychain are cleared the next time SecureChat is installed, and a new install starts over with a new account. The old account record stays on the server until it has been unused for 12 months.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the EU General Data Protection Regulation or the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection), you can ask for access to, correction, deletion or a copy of your data, restrict or object to how it's used, and complain to your data protection authority. As the server keeps nothing that names you, we can act only on a record we can find; Delete Account in the app always works. Write to securechat.private.messenger at proton.me.
